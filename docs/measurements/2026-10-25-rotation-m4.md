# Sideways portrait photos: effect on search quality

Date: 2026-10-25. Machine: MacBook Air M4. Same 678 stock photos, 27 judged English queries
and their Polish translations as [the M4 model comparison](2026-10-24-model-comparison-m4.md).
Script: [`rotate_eval.py`](../../spikes/model-eval/rotate_eval.py).

Question: if a decoder ignores the EXIF Orientation tag, portrait photos taken with a phone
are embedded sideways. How much does that cost in search results? Pillow does not apply the
tag on open, so any pipeline that skips `ImageOps.exif_transpose` has this problem; it
also applies to the JPEG embedded in RAW files, which carries no orientation of its own
([N305 notes](2026-10-25-n305-family-library.md)).

## Method

- The 75 portrait photos of the set (long edge vertical after decoding) are embedded three
  ways: upright, rotated 90° counter-clockwise (how a phone stores them with Orientation 6)
  and rotated 90° clockwise (Orientation 8). The other 603 photos keep their vectors.
- Scoring as before: nP@8 and MRR against the existing judgements. 12 of the 27 queries
  have at least one relevant portrait photo (35 of 214 relevant marks).
- Photos that enter a top 8 only after rotation were never judged and count as not
  relevant. Up to 14 such hits per model and language (out of 216 top-8 slots), so the
  rotated scores can be slightly too low. The number of relevant portrait photos in the top
  8 does not depend on that.
- The upright run reproduces the published numbers to three decimals.

## Results

Cosine similarity between the upright and the sideways embedding of the same photo:

| Model | Median | 10th percentile | Min |
|---|---|---|---|
| SigLIP 2 B/16-224 | 0.925–0.929 | 0.891–0.895 | 0.800 |
| SigLIP 2 B/16-256 | 0.931–0.934 | 0.899–0.903 | 0.786 |
| XLM-R B/32 | 0.870 | 0.787–0.793 | 0.700 |

Search quality, upright → Orientation 6 / Orientation 8:

| Model | Lang | nP@8, all 27 queries | nP@8, 12 affected queries | Relevant portrait photos in top 8 | Queries worse / better |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SigLIP 2 B/16-224 | EN | 0.838 → 0.834 / 0.851 | 0.793 → 0.782 / 0.793 | 18 → 21 / 20 | 2/1, 1/2 |
| | PL | 0.820 → 0.815 / 0.815 | 0.762 → 0.751 / 0.751 | 17 → 15 / 15 | 1/0, 1/0 |
| SigLIP 2 B/16-256 | EN | 0.857 → 0.865 / 0.865 | 0.803 → 0.794 / 0.793 | 20 → 19 / 20 | 2/2, 1/1 |
| | PL | 0.817 → 0.816 / 0.825 | 0.751 → 0.748 / 0.769 | 13 → 15 / 17 | 1/1, 1/2 |
| XLM-R B/32 | EN | 0.882 → 0.868 / 0.868 | 0.864 → 0.831 / 0.831 | 18 → 14 / 16 | 3/0, 3/0 |
| | PL | 0.854 → 0.821 / 0.840 | 0.805 → 0.772 / 0.772 | 18 → 14 / 14 | 3/0, 2/0 |

MRR moved by 0.041 at most, in both directions, with no consistent pattern.

- **SigLIP 2 barely notices.** Sideways embeddings stay close to the upright ones (median
  cosine about 0.93), and every change in nP@8 is within ±0.02, in both directions. With one
  judge and 27 queries that is noise.
- **XLM-R B/32 loses consistently.** The sideways embedding is further away (median 0.87),
  3.3 points nP@8 on the affected queries in both languages, fewer relevant portrait photos
  in the top 8 (18 → 14–16), and no query got better.
- The set is stock photography with 11 % portrait photos. A phone library has more portrait
  photos, so the share of affected queries is likely higher there; the per-photo effect
  above is what carries over.

## Consequences

- For XLM-R B/32, Sightline's default, orientation matters: the RAW path must rotate the
  embedded JPEG by the RAW container's orientation, as the N305 notes already say.
- For SigLIP 2 the search cost of a missing transpose is small on this set. The other cost of
  a missing transpose stays: width and height reported for a sideways photo are swapped.
